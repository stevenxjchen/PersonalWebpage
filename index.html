Descent · Featured work

Runway Overrun Awareness and Alerting System

A research project with civil-aviation partners on a system that warns pilots when a landing may run past the end of the runway. My part was the alert design: when the cockpit warns, how loudly, and when it stays quiet.

  1. 01 · Bound

    Find the stopping range

    From overrun accident records and landing-performance data, I split the inputs into those fixed before touchdown and those the pilot can still change. Best and worst combinations of the adjustable ones give a shortest and longest stop.

    Fixed before touchdown
    • Weight
    • Elevation
    • Wind
    • Slope
    • Temperature
    • Approach speed
    • Surface
    Pilot adjusts
    • Brakes
    • Reverse thrust
    • Anti-ice
  2. 02 · Decide

    Write the alert rules

    With the runway-risk margin as input, I wrote the alert behaviour as conditional rules across the three alert levels used in aviation standards, each with its own light and sound. A go-around switches alerts off to avoid false alarms.

  3. 03 · Test

    Run it in a simulator

    I ran the rules through typical landing cases in a full-flight simulator (different speeds, wet runways, short runway left), checked when each level fired and how accurately, and helped write up what needed changing.
